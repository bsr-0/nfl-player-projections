The range matters more than the number. The bar shows the 80% interval: the outcome lands inside it about four times in five. The white tick is the point projection; for a played week the green (inside the range) or red (outside) block is what actually happened. Measured prediction spread is roughly half the spread of real outcomes, so a single number understates how high a good week can go — two players with the same projection and different ranges are not the same bet.

What the number is made of. Each projection is the weekly model shrunk toward the player's season pace (the season model's projected total ÷ 17) by games played this season: w = games / (games + 3) on the weekly model, the rest on the pace. The purple share of the small bar is pace, the blue share is in-season form. Before a player's first game the number is the pace (tagged no games yet); by game 8 it is three-quarters weekly model. Hover the bar for both inputs. Measured on a full replay of 2025, this shrinkage cut average error from 4.35 to 4.24 points and helped at every stage of the season.

What changes between weeks. Each week uses that player's real opponent from the schedule, the injury report as of that week, and only the games played before it. Players on bye are omitted for that week.

Team / opponent and compare. The team dropdown shows either side of one NFL game — pick a team to see it and its opponent together. Check the box on up to 4 rows to compare them side by side (the panel appears above the table); the selection is week-scoped and clears when you change weeks, since it's a "how do these two look this week" question.
